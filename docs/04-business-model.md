# 04 — Business model

*Sources: investor teaser, two-page pitch, the "win-win-win" case study, the full concept
paper (all January 2026) and a second, earlier concept note for an insolvency-management
platform. Figures are the founders' targets and estimates, not results.*

## Revenue streams

| Stream | Target share | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Platform subscription (B2C) | ~70 % | 600–899 EUR per debtor |
| Lawyer licence (B2B) | ~20 % | monthly platform fee for partner lawyers (100–500 EUR/month), plus a per-mandate commission of 100–500 EUR in the legal-structure draft |
| White label (B2B) | ~10 % | licence for debt-counselling offices |

A separate concept note for the pure **case-management software** (selling the tool to
firms instead of serving debtors) suggested: per case 50–150 EUR/month, or per firm
500–2,000 EUR/month, with "time saved per insolvency case" as the value proposition.

| Metric | Target |

| LTV : CAC | 5 : 1 |
| Payback period | 3 months |
| Gross margin | 70 %+ |

## Scaling logic (flywheel)

Software economics: high initial development cost, low variable cost per additional user.

```
more users → more data → better automation → lower cost per case
     ↑                                               ↓
     └──── more users ← cheaper prices ←─────────────┘
```

## Go-to-market

**Target group 1 — debtors (B2C).** Acquisition channels from the pitch: TikTok (short,
emotional videos about debt and a fresh start), Instagram (storytelling, success stories,
education), Facebook (targeted ads), Google SEO/SEA. Conversion: free financial analysis as
lead magnet → trust through transparent information → free consultation as the end of the
lead process → conversion to the paid subscription (50 EUR/month).

**Target group 2 — partner lawyers (B2B).** Career starters and small firms that need
mandates; no insolvency specialisation needed ("every lawyer can deliver all necessary
services after a 4-day training"). Value: fully prepared files, minimal admin, plannable
recurring income, access to a new client segment.

In practice the project started with a regional law-firm website in Berlin as "phase 1"
(regional SEO, district landing pages) and kept the national platform brand for "phase 2" —
see [11](11-marketing-and-seo.md).

## Case study: "win-win-win"

The pitch's illustrative case. Starting point: **25,000 EUR** of debt with various
creditors; the debtor pays 500 EUR/month uncoordinated to several collection agencies; his
remaining income does not cover living costs, the overdraft grows, bills go unpaid. Debts
grow despite all efforts; eventually he capitulates and stops paying.

**Scenario A — without the platform.** Dunning, collection, enforcement. Within months
debt rises through fees, default interest, collection costs and bailiff fees to **35,000 EUR+**.
In the end only consumer insolvency remains: at least 3,000 EUR procedure costs over three
years. With a garnishable income of 100 EUR/month he pays 3,600 EUR, almost entirely
consumed by the procedure costs.

**Scenario B — with the platform.** First measure: stop all payments. The 500 EUR stay with
him; he can afford the platform subscription; the overdraft stops growing. In a
stabilisation phase of about six months all liabilities are captured, the economic situation
is analysed and a settlement plan is prepared. Creditors are informed from the start, which
stops the cost avalanche. Result: settlement of **12,000 EUR in total, payable over 48 months
at 250 EUR** (quotas above 40 % are considered very good in the industry). He repays less than
half and still has 250 EUR more per month than before.

| | Without | With |
|---|---|---|
| Initial liabilities | 25,000 EUR | 25,000 EUR |
| Monthly burden before | 500 EUR (not sustainable) | 500 EUR (not sustainable) |
| Course | capitulation | structured settlement |
| Debt development | rises to 35,000 EUR+ | frozen at 25,000 EUR |
| Outcome | consumer insolvency | out-of-court settlement |
| Cost for the debtor | 3,000 EUR procedure costs | 900 EUR (subscription + lawyer) |
| Monthly payment | 150 EUR (garnishable) | 250 EUR (voluntary, sustainable) |
| Duration | 36 months + credit-bureau entry | 48 months, then debt-free |
| Paid to creditors | approx. 1,200 EUR | 12,000 EUR |
| Quota for creditors | under 4 % | 48 % |
| Credit rating | destroyed | potentially preserved |
| Procedure | public insolvency | discreet agreement |

| Party | Without | With |
|---|---|---|
| Debtor | 4+ years lost, credit destroyed, stigma | saves 13,000 EUR, keeps dignity and credit, clear situation |
| Creditors | approx. 1,200 EUR (under 4 %) | 12,000 EUR (48 %) |
| Platform | — | 600 EUR subscription |
| Partner lawyer | — | possibly 600 EUR fee |
| Courts | burdened by insolvency proceedings | relieved |
| Society | bears the follow-up costs of over-indebtedness | economic capacity preserved |

> The source is not internally consistent: its prose says creditors would get "at best
> 600 EUR, under two percent" in scenario A and that they receive "8 times" as much in
> scenario B, while the table says approx. 1,200 EUR / under 4 % and "20× more"; the
> garnishable amount is 100 EUR/month in the prose and 150 EUR in the table. Reproduced as written.

Conclusion of the case study: the debtor would have capitulated in both scenarios. The
question is not *whether* he stops paying but *what happens afterwards*.
